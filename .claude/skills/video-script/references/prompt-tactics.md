# Prompt tactics — copy these word for word

These are the exact prompts that make the script. Use them on the AI (or on yourself) while writing.

---

## 1. Cold open (the anti-hook)

> "Write a 120–160 word cold open. Start with a cinematic scene, list 3–5 unsettling physical details, then reveal a single sentence that reframes the entire story. End on a question that forces a binary interpretation."

**Binary question** = only two possible answers. "Madness, or real danger?" "A curse, or a bad contract?"
Two options, not five. The brain must pick a side — so it stays.

---

## 2. Chapters

> "Create 12–18 chapters. Each chapter must have: a headline that implies conflict + a first sentence that re-hooks."

Swap the number for the video length: one chapter per 90–120 seconds. 6–8 for a 12-minute video; 12–18 is for the 60–100 minute episodes.

Chapter headline: never a label ("The Early Years"). Always a small conflict ("The Architect Who Refused To Stop").

---

## 3. Scale paragraph (the numbers section)

> "Insert a 'scale paragraph' every 8–12 minutes: 4–8 verified numbers that quantify the madness."

Example: *"One hundred and sixty rooms. Forty staircases. Ten thousand windows. Two thousand doors — and some of them opened onto nothing but air."*

Every number must be **verified**. Put the source in the script.

---

## 4. Ambiguity

> "For every paranormal claim, include one grounded explanation and leave the verdict open."

Never say the myth is true. Never crush it either. Present it as what people *said*, then give the practical explanation, then move on.

---

## 5. Two-layer script

> "Write for two audiences at once: 60% eerie narrative + 40% historical contextualization."

Paranormal crowd = seances, bells, curses. History crowd = economics, wages, payroll, the Depression, charity, illness, architecture.

Dual-intent packaging: it **looks** spooky (that gets the click), it **delivers** real history (that keeps the trust).

---

## 6. Full script pass (after the outline exists)

> "Expand this outline into a 12-minute narration, 145 words per minute, calm documentary tone for US viewers aged 55+. Short sentences. No greeting, no hype words, no 'top 10', no 'as you can see'. Concrete details over adjectives. One image idea per 50 words."
