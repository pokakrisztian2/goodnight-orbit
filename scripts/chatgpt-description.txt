Fall asleep while Otto, an astronaut on the night shift, slowly explains the most confusing parts of ChatGPT: tokens, the map of meaning, attention, training on trillions of words, choosing the next word, learning manners, memory, why it makes things up, thinking step by step, and what scientists see when they look inside.

Otto asks the machine one question tonight: why is the sky dark at night? We follow that question all the way through the machine, from Joseph Weizenbaum's little ELIZA program in 1966, to eight Google researchers and a paper called Attention Is All You Need, to an AI that briefly believed it was the Golden Gate Bridge.

You don't need to understand all of it. Nobody fully does yet. If you fall asleep halfway, that's the plan.
