Fall asleep while Otto, an astronaut on the night shift, slowly explains the most confusing parts of electricity: what it actually is, volts and amps, why electrons crawl slower than a snail, how magnets make electricity, why light is electric too, the power grid, lightning, and the electricity inside your own body.

From rubbed amber in ancient Greece to a whole French village running through the hail in 1752, from Franklin's kite and his Christmas turkey to a poor bookbinder named Faraday, from Edison's first power station to oven clocks across Europe running six minutes slow, and the night's question: if electrons are so slow, why does the lamp come on instantly?

You don't need to understand all of it. If you fall asleep halfway, that's the plan.
