So how does a cloud make lightning? Inside a big storm cloud, the air is rising very fast. It carries little crystals of ice up, and up, while heavier pieces of hail tumble around lower down. And as they're tossed about together, they become charged.

The light ice crystals end up positive, and are carried up to the top of the cloud. The heavier hail ends up negative, lower down. And the two kinds of charge pull apart, further and further. The air between them is a good insulator. It holds them back. Until, at last, the difference is too great. The air gives way. And all that charge rushes across in one bright flash.

All the storms on Earth, together, are part of one great circuit. At any moment, about two thousand thunderstorms are rolling over the planet. And together, they work like a battery. They keep the ground slightly negative, and the high air slightly positive.
