Our story starts long ago, in ancient Greece, with a man called Thales, and a piece of amber.

Amber is old tree resin that has hardened into something like a warm, golden stone. If you rub it, on a sleeve, or on a bit of wool, something odd happens. It starts to pull little light things toward it. The way a balloon, rubbed on your hair, will make your hair stand up and reach for it.

But that word didn't arrive for about two thousand years. It came from an Englishman named William Gilbert. In sixteen hundred, he wrote a big book about magnets, in Latin. And to describe the strange pull of rubbed amber, he made up a new Latin word. Electricus. It means, like amber.
