# STRATEGY.md — why this channel works

This is the thinking behind the channel. Read this before you make anything.

---

## 1. Who we talk to

**Old people in the USA. 55+. Often 65+.**

Why them:

- They watch the **whole video**. Young people skip after 30 seconds. Old people sit and watch.
- High watch time → YouTube shows the video to more people. Free push.
- They watch **on the TV**, not the phone.
- Ads aimed at them cost a lot: insurance, medicine, banks, cruises, houses. So **RPM is high** (RPM = money per 1000 views).

---

## 2. We do not make "content". We make **background cinema**.

This is the biggest idea in the whole project.

Our videos are **NOT** meant to be:

- ❌ intensely watched
- ❌ paused
- ❌ rewatched for the details

They **are** meant to be:

- ✅ played on the TV
- ✅ played while cooking
- ✅ played while resting
- ✅ played late at night

That is called **lean-back** watching. The viewer leans back. They do not click. They let it run.

**Why YouTube loves this:**

YouTube's biggest metric is **"time spent on platform per user"** — not views. Lean-back content:

- boosts **session length** (they stay on YouTube longer)
- reduces **exits** (they do not close the app)
- increases **autoplay chains** (the next video starts, and they let it)

So YouTube pushes us. Not because we are exciting. Because we **keep people on YouTube**.

**Practical rule:** the voice must carry the whole story. Never say "look at this" or "as you can see". If the story stops working with your eyes closed, it is broken.

---

## 3. The format

- **Long videos: 12–20 minutes.** Over 8 minutes we can put ads inside. Also less competition — most AI channels make short stuff.
- **Slideshow of AI images.** Slow zoom on each picture. Cheap, fast, and it fits the calm mood.
- **Evergreen, not news.** News videos die in 3 days. Ours live for years and keep earning.

---

## 4. The feeling (this is the engine)

The video must trigger:

**Loss. Destruction. Forgotten history. Nostalgia.**

Things that are gone. Places that fell apart. A way of life that died. A family that lost everything.

Old viewers feel this in the chest. That feeling keeps them to the end.

Not "wow amazing". Not "you won't believe". Just: *"...it's all gone now."*

---

## 5. Anti-design (the look)

**Anti-design is NOT:**

- ❌ bad design
- ❌ low effort
- ❌ just a plain lazy thumbnail

**Anti-design IS:** on purpose saying no to how YouTube looks today.

Today every thumbnail shouts: extreme contrast, huge faces, huge emotions, red arrows, circles, emojis. Visual shouting. Everybody shouts, so nobody hears. Eyes slide past. (Banner blindness.)

**We whisper.**

> We refuse to shout → we whisper... to the right people.
> That is already a **positioning move**.

### The law: one object, one idea, one emotion

Anti-design follows:

- **one visual anchor**
- **one emotional tone**
- **one intellectual promise**

**No collage. No chaos.**

Why: the brain reads it instantly · no decision fatigue · no confusion.

> **Confusion kills clicks more than boredom.**
> "Most thumbnails fail not because they're boring — but because they're **too busy**."

### Why it works with the algorithm

Anti-design gives:

- lower but **stable** CTR
- **very high watch time**
- low bounce rate
- long sessions
- frequent returns

YouTube reads that as: *"This content satisfies viewers."*

**Satisfaction beats excitement.** Nobody ever feels "you cheated me with that clickbait thumbnail". They click **deliberately** — so they watch **longer**.

The feeling we want: **a museum, not TikTok.**

That does two things: we **stand out in the feed**, and we look like **intelligent content**. People think: *"This looks serious. This isn't junk. I'll give it a watch."*

Same rule inside the video: calm voice, slow pace, no sound effects, no jump cuts.

---

## 6. Compounding: each video makes the NEXT one stronger

Most channels are **linear**: video 40 is worth the same as video 1.

Ours is **compounding**.

After one video, the viewer learns:

- a new **era**
- a new **class** of people
- a new **pattern** (rise → excess → fall)

So the next video feels: *"I already understand this world better."*

That is huge. **People do not just watch content — they watch worlds they feel fluent in.**

We are not making 100 separate videos. We are building **one micro-universe**.

How we do it:

- Stay in the same world: same country, same decades, same kind of people.
- Reuse the same words for the same things.
- Reference earlier videos in one calm line.
- Never explain everything from zero. Reward the regulars.

## 7. Execution — what actually makes a good video

- good AI image prompts
- real storytelling
- **3-block structure**
- **micro-hooks** at the end of each block
- and the opening: the **anti-hook**

### The anti-hook = a cinematic cold open

No greeting. No "hey guys". No "in this video". Open like a **movie trailer**.

1. **Sensory scene** — "Midnight in California. Fog on the lawn. A crooked house with too many windows."
2. **Stack strange visual details, fast** — "Stairs that climb into a ceiling. Doors that open into a wall. Windows facing other windows."
3. **The core thesis in one line** — "She believed that if she ever stopped building, she would die."

Why it works:

- It is a movie trailer, not a YouTube intro.
- It triggers **mental imagery instantly** — perfect for TV, where they listen more than look.
- It opens a **question loop in 20 seconds**: madness, or real danger? They must stay for the answer.

### Chapters do algorithm work

One chapter every 90–120 seconds — 6–8 in a 12-minute video, 12–18 in a long-form one. They:

1. make even a 90-minute video feel **digestible**
2. give **mini-starts** to viewers who join late
3. create **re-entry points** — people come back the next day

**Long videos die when they feel endless.** Chapters are psychological checkpoints.

### The numbers section = dopamine for adults

Every 8–12 minutes, drop 4–8 verified numbers: *"160 rooms. 40 staircases. 10,000 windows. 2,000 doors."*

Older viewers love concrete facts. Numbers make it feel **documentary-real**. And "160 rooms" becomes the title and the thumbnail.

### Ambiguity on purpose

Present the folklore → quietly offer the practical explanation → **never fully resolve it**.

The viewer stays to find the real answer. The myth survives (myth sells). The comments stay alive for years.
We never state a myth as fact — we say who claimed it. Honest, and still open.

### Two-layer audience: ghost people + history people

- **paranormal crowd:** seances, bells, curses
- **history crowd:** economics, wages, payroll, the Depression, charity, illness, architecture

**60% eerie narrative + 40% historical context.**

*Dual-intent packaging:* it **looks** spooky, so people click. It **delivers** real history, so people trust and return.

That is why it scales faster than a pure ghost channel. Two audiences, one video. And the history half is exactly where the premium ad money sits.

Full rules and copy-paste prompts: the `video-script` skill.

## 8. Topics that bring rich advertisers

Ads pay more when the topic sits near money:

- Real estate / big houses / land
- Finance / old family fortunes
- Insurance
- Travel

A video about **an old mansion and the family that lost it** earns more than a video about cartoons. Same views, more money.

---

## 9. Why now

A good 12-minute picture video used to take days. AI images + AI voice make it hours. Almost everybody points that at loud, young, trendy content. Almost nobody points it at **quiet, slow, long content for old people.**

That is the gap.

---

## 10. What we do NOT do

- No multiple accounts, no proxies, no fake "aged" accounts. One real channel. Those tricks get channels killed and we do not need them.
- No copying other channels' scripts or footage.
- No hiding that we use AI. YouTube asks; we tick the box.
- No spamming 5 near-identical videos a day. YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule kills the money for that. Fewer, better videos.
