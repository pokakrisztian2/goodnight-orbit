## What this tells us

1. **Deep past wins.** Early humans, the Ice Age, "before civilization", dinosaurs. It is history AND science at the same time. Perfect for our channel.
2. **Cold, survival, cozy.** "Sleeping in castles without freezing" — warmth vs cold is a sleep-friendly feeling.
3. **Viewers hate AI slop.** "No AI" and "Non-AI" are in winning titles. So our videos must NOT look like cheap AI.
4. **Length: 2–2.5 hours** is normal for the winners.
5. The Gilded Age mansion idea did **not** show up in the winners. Move it later.

## Decision for video 1

**"Life in the Ice Age: What People Did All Winter, 20,000 Years Ago | Calm History for Sleep"**

- Deep past (proven topic) + cold/fire/shelter (cozy) + science (climate, animals).
- Not a copy: we pick one season and one group of people, and walk through it slowly.
- Visuals move well: fire, snow falling, breath in cold air, stars.

## How we avoid looking like AI slop

- **Real old paintings and museum art** (public domain = free to use, no owner). Many museums give free images: The Met Open Access, Smithsonian Open Access, Rijksmuseum, Wikimedia Commons. Old paintings of prehistoric life (e.g. Charles R. Knight, before 1929) are free in the USA.
- **Real photos of real places**: cave art, fossils, landscapes — only free-license ones (CC0 / public domain / CC-BY with credit).
- **AI only for motion**: short 5–10 second loops made fresh for each video (fire, snow, stars). Never reuse loops across videos.
- **Real research + our own script** every time, sources in the description.
- Always check the license of each picture. Write it in `assets/images/<video>/CREDITS.md`.

## Example the user sent: Lights Out Physics (checked 2026-09-27)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UrHz56rppN4 — "What Actually Controls the Speed of Sound?" — 118 min.
Channel: 38 videos, 5,050 subs. Every video ~110–120 min. Physics only.

What they do so it does NOT look mass-produced:

1. **One real question per video**, with a real answer at the end. "Where does sound get its speed from? By the end of tonight, you will know."
2. **Very long, real script**: ~18,300 words for 2 hours. It is a real explanation with a path, not a list of facts.
3. **Story scenes**: "A storm is coming... count after the flash." Sensory, calm, second person.
4. **12 named chapters** ("Newton's wrong number", "Laplace hears the heat").
5. **14 real sources** in the description: universities, NASA, arXiv, Scientific American.
6. **A disclaimer**: "education and relaxation channel... based on peer-reviewed research... speculation is marked."
7. **Also on Spotify** as a podcast. More places = more of a real brand.
8. Subscribe ask in the first 2 minutes.
9. Thumbnail: glowing science image on black + 3 big words ("WHAT MOVES SOUND?").
